# Validation Reflection — gelkhoda (validating sbombry1)

Before discussion, agreement was high for the structural fields and low for the interpretive ones. HasRecovered matched in all 20 comparisons (κ = 1.0), since both of us read it directly off the post-gap commit count. Currentstatus agreed 90% (κ = 0.62) and ActivityPattern 75% (κ = 0.62). The free-text and multi-label fields were much weaker: WhyRecovered agreed 50% and RecentThemes 45%. After adjudication all 28 disagreements were resolved. The pattern is typical of qualitative coding: when the codebook gives a mechanical rule (a date cutoff, yes/no), raters converge. When it asks for a reason or a theme, each rater's vocabulary and level of detail diverge.

The adjudicated labels show consistent signals before long gaps. Most gaps follow a release or version bump: FairRoot after v19.0.0, tableone after a version bump, posteriordb after v0.2.0, nexpy after a version update, enkf-c between patch releases. Others follow documentation clean-up (gabinsane, LinkedGeoData's imprint and download pages) or a thesis or paper being finished (arfc/pride, quisp). A release followed by README polishing is the clearest sign that a quiet period is coming. Recovery, by contrast, was almost never "just a break". It had a visible trigger: an upstream dependency change (matplotlib 3.6 for nexpy, pandas Copy-on-Write for DAME, CRAN or roxygen checks for optmatch), a new contributor bringing a new direction (vimpute in VIM, pytest/GitHub Actions in tableone), or a new upstream release to support (Apollo 7.0).

A gap also often does not mean the project died. VIM, optmatch, tableone and pymap3d each had 8–20 month gaps and are active in 2026. Many scientific packages are effectively finished and only wake up when their ecosystem changes. Conversely, a few post-gap commits do not mean recovery either. gabinsane's single-day burst and arfc/pride's merge-only activity counted as recent activity for Currentstatus but not as recovery, and arfc/pride was relabeled "stopped".

Four interpretation pitfalls recurred. First, vague reasons such as "simple break" or "maintenance done behind commits" are not supported by evidence. We resolved these by requiring each WhyRecovered value to cite the first post-gap commits, and often combined the author's gap cause with the validator's recovery trigger as a compromise. Second, the steady vs declining vs irregular boundary is fuzzy on plots with spikes. Yearly totals settled FairRoot (declining from its 2022 peak) and VIM and tableone (irregular). Third, Currentstatus was sometimes judged by feel rather than by the 2025-04-01 rule. Fourth, RecentThemes coding of merge commits, version-tag commits and bot deploys was inconsistent. We agreed that merges are Other, bare version tags are Release, and gh-pages or dependabot commits are Automated bot contributions. A codebook improvement would be to state these three conventions explicitly and to require WhyRecovered to quote at least one post-gap commit.
